Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed inside email-security pipelines, raising the question of how they behave not just on benign messages but on adversarially crafted phishing content. We present LLM-PEA, an evaluation framework that probes GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet 4, and Grok-3 under three prompting strategies (structured, zero-shot, chain-of-thought) across a clean baseline and three attack dimensions: adversarial rephrasing, prompt injection, and cross-lingual translation. The three models attain 95%, 94%, and 88% accuracy on the balanced baseline, but susceptibility depends on the attack. Adversarial rephrasing of 189 matched phishing samples yields attack success rates of 4.2% (GPT-4o), 12.7% (Claude Sonnet 4), and 0% (Grok-3); a separate single-template instruction-override on 1,000 original emails yields 4.2%, 1.3%, and 12.3% respectively—a different susceptibility ordering. Applying six injection templates to the same 189 rephrased samples yields 1.1%, 2.9%, and 1.6%, together with the adversarial-only results covering three of four cells of a 2×2 factorial on matched inputs. The sharpest degradation is cross-lingual: Claude Sonnet 4’s false-positive rate rises from 2.4% in English to a mean of 24.1% across Bangla, Chinese, and Hindi (a +904% relative increase), peaking at 30.6% in Bangla and erasing its baseline advantage. All runs use temperature 0; adversarial rephrases are generated by GPT-4o, itself one of the evaluated detectors. No single model is uniformly robust across these three attack dimensions.
